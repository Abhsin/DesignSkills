# User Modeling: FocusFlow

## Context

**Problem:** Remote workers lose 3+ hours daily to distractions and context-switching
**Research basis:** 8 user interviews with remote workers, analysis of r/WFH Reddit threads

---

## Persona 1: The Distracted Developer

*Mid-career software engineer who can't resist "just checking" Twitter between tasks*

**Goals:**
- Ship features on time without late-night catch-up work
- Maintain focus through complex debugging sessions
- Prove remote work is working (worried about RTO mandates)

**Context:**
- Works from 1-bedroom apartment, desk in bedroom
- Lives alone, controls environment but lacks external accountability
- Gets distracted by Twitter, Reddit, YouTube, news sites
- Peak focus hours: 9am-11am (before lunch derails concentration)

**Pain points:**
- Opens Twitter "for 2 minutes" and loses 45 minutes
- Forgets what task they were doing after distraction
- Works until 8pm to make up for distracted morning
- Guilt spiral: distracted → feel bad → more distracted to avoid guilt

**Current behavior:**
- Uses Forest app occasionally (abandons when it gets annoying)
- Has Freedom installed but disabled most of the time
- Keeps phone in other room (helps but not enough)
- Tries "just one more task before break" (never works)

**Constraints:**
- Budget: Prefers free, would pay $5-10/month if proven value
- Time: Won't spend >2 minutes setting up per day
- Flexibility: Needs to access "blocked" sites occasionally for work research
- Learning curve: Must work immediately, no onboarding

**What success looks like:**
- Complete 4-hour deep work block without checking Twitter
- End workday at 5pm instead of 8pm
- Ship features on original estimates

**Quote:** *"I know I'm wasting time, I just can't seem to stop myself. I need something that doesn't let me cheat my way out of focus mode."*

## Persona 2: The Overwhelmed Parent

*Parent of young kids trying to work during naptime and after bedtime*

**Goals:**
- Maximize productive output during limited available hours
- Not work after kids' bedtime (preserve family time)
- Keep job while kids are too young for full-time daycare

**Context:**
- Works from kitchen table or bedroom while kids nap/play
- Has 2-3 hour windows of focus time (unpredictable schedule)
- Gets distracted by household tasks ("I should start laundry...")
- Interrupted frequently by kids (even during "focus" time)

**Pain points:**
- Feels constant urgency to maximize nap time productivity
- Guilt about working when kids are awake
- Guilt about household chores when focusing on work
- Context-switching between work and parenting destroys focus

**Current behavior:**
- Uses timers to track available work time
- Physically moves to different room to "get in work mode"
- Leaves phone on silent (misses kid-related calls)
- Works in short bursts (30-45 min max before interruption)

**Constraints:**
- Budget: Free tier essential (single income during parental leave)
- Time: Absolutely no time for setup or configuration
- Interruptions: Must gracefully handle unexpected breaks
- Device: Desktop/laptop only (phone reserved for kid emergencies)

**What success looks like:**
- Complete work during kids' naptime instead of after bedtime
- Actually finish tasks instead of half-done fragments
- Feel less guilty about both work and parenting

**Quote:** *"I have maybe 2 hours total to work each day. I can't waste 30 minutes of that scrolling Instagram. I need to make every minute count."*

## Persona 3: The Procrastinating Writer

*Freelance writer who battles resistance at the start of every writing session*

**Goals:**
- Start writing sessions without 2-hour procrastination warm-up
- Meet article deadlines consistently (currently late 50% of the time)
- Build sustainable writing routine that doesn't rely on last-minute panic

**Context:**
- Works from various locations (home, coffee shops, library)
- Flexible schedule (blessing and curse)
- Gets distracted by "research" that turns into YouTube rabbit holes
- Writes best in morning but procrastinates until afternoon panic

**Pain points:**
- Knows exactly what to write but can't make themselves start
- "Just need to research a bit more" = 2 hours of YouTube
- Opening blank document triggers avoidance behaviors
- Deadlines are the only thing that creates urgency (unhealthy)

**Current behavior:**
- Uses WriteOrDie app for forcing word count
- Goes to coffee shop to "force" focus (expensive, inconsistent)
- Promises self rewards for finishing ("after this section, I can check Twitter")
- All-nighters before deadlines (only time procrastination stops working)

**Constraints:**
- Budget: Paid tools are fine if they solve the problem ($15-20/month max)
- Mobility: Works in different locations, needs cloud-based solution
- Writing tools: Needs to work with Google Docs, Notion, Scrivener
- Emergency escape: Must allow "urgent" access to blocked sites (for client messages)

**What success looks like:**
- Start writing within 15 minutes of sitting down
- Meet deadlines without all-nighters
- Consistent daily word count (500+ words)

**Quote:** *"I'm not lazy, I'm just really good at convincing myself that procrastination is productive. I need something that calls out my BS."*

## Scenarios

### Persona 1 Scenarios

**Scenario 1.1: Morning Focus Session**
- **Situation:** It's 9am Monday, coffee made, sitting at desk ready to tackle complex bug
- **Trigger:** Opens laptop, sees Twitter bookmark in browser
- **Goal:** Work uninterrupted for 90 minutes on the bug
- **Current approach:** Promises self "no Twitter" → checks "just once" at 9:15am → loses focus by 9:45am → frustrated rest of morning
- **Frustration:** "I ruined my best focus hours AGAIN"

**Scenario 1.2: Post-Lunch Productivity Dip**
- **Situation:** 1:30pm, back from lunch, energy low, easy to get distracted
- **Trigger:** Sit down at desk, multiple browser tabs from morning still open
- **Goal:** Review code before 3pm standup
- **Current approach:** "I'll just watch one YouTube video to wake up" → 45 minutes gone → rushed code review → miss issues
- **Frustration:** "I'm most vulnerable after lunch. I know it's coming and still can't stop it."

### Persona 2 Scenarios

**Scenario 2.1: Naptime Work Sprint**
- **Situation:** Baby just fell asleep for 1.5-hour nap, need to complete report
- **Trigger:** Urgency to maximize limited time, stress about wasting naptime
- **Goal:** Finish report draft before baby wakes up
- **Current approach:** Sits down → sees kitchen mess → "I should just clean up first" → 20 minutes cleaning → baby wakes up → report not done
- **Frustration:** "I wasted the ONE productive window I had today on dishes. Now I have to work until midnight."

**Scenario 2.2: Evening Work After Kids' Bedtime**
- **Situation:** 8:30pm, kids finally asleep, exhausted but need to finish work
- **Trigger:** Know this is last chance to work, but mentally drained
- **Goal:** Complete 2 hours of work before 10:30pm bedtime
- **Current approach:** Opens laptop → "too tired to think" → mindless social media scrolling → 10pm → panic work → midnight finish
- **Frustration:** "I sacrificed family time to work and then wasted it scrolling TikTok. I'm failing at both work and parenting."

### Persona 3 Scenarios

**Scenario 3.1: Article Deadline Tomorrow**
- **Situation:** Article due tomorrow 9am, haven't started, 800 words needed
- **Trigger:** Panic sets in, can no longer procrastinate
- **Goal:** Write entire article in one session
- **Current approach:** Midnight → writes 800 words in 90 minutes → miserable but done → unhealthy pattern
- **Frustration:** "Why can I only write when panicking? This is unsustainable but it's the only thing that works."

**Scenario 3.2: "Research" Rabbit Hole**
- **Situation:** 10am, coffee made, outline ready, time to write
- **Trigger:** First sentence needs a fact-check
- **Goal:** Write 500-word section
- **Current approach:** Opens Google → finds interesting tangent → "this might be useful" → 2 hours later → original question forgotten
- **Frustration:** "I know I'm not actually researching anymore, I'm procrastinating. But it FEELS productive so I don't stop."

## Key Insights

### Commonalities
*These are table-stakes features—all personas need these*

- **Blocking that actually works:** Can't easily disable mid-session
- **Flexible session lengths:** 30 min to 3+ hours (different attention spans)
- **Desktop-first:** All do primary work on laptops
- **Minimal setup:** Must work immediately, no configuration
- **Emergency escape hatch:** Occasional legitimate need to access blocked sites

### Divergences
*These inform prioritization and feature flags*

**Persona 1 (Developer):**
- Needs long, uninterrupted blocks (90+ min)
- Primary distraction: social media and news
- Would pay for proven value
- Wants data/analytics (how much time saved)

**Persona 2 (Parent):**
- Needs to handle interruptions gracefully
- Primary distraction: household tasks (mental, not digital)
- Must have free tier
- Wants simple "start focus" button (no complexity)

**Persona 3 (Writer):**
- Needs "start session" friction removed
- Primary distraction: fake research
- Already pays for tools, willing to pay here too
- Wants word count integration or writing-specific features

### Design Implications

1. **Blocking must be strict but escapable**
   - Developer: "Hard block with 5-minute bypass cooldown"
   - Parent: "Soft reminders, easy disable (for kid emergencies)"
   - Writer: "Shame-based messaging when bypassing ('Are you sure?')"

2. **Session flexibility is critical**
   - Parent can't commit to 90-minute sessions
   - Developer needs long blocks
   - Writer needs variable length
   - **Solution:** Presets (30/60/90 min) + custom duration

3. **Free tier must solve core problem**
   - Parent can't pay
   - If free tier works, Developer will upgrade for analytics
   - Writer will pay if it actually stops procrastination

4. **Household distraction tracking**
   - Parent's distractions aren't digital
   - **Maybe:** Simple "I got distracted by [chore/other]" log
   - **Or:** Accept that household distractions are out of scope for MVP

5. **Emergency bypass**
   - All personas mentioned legitimate need to disable
   - **Solution:** 5-minute cooldown + "Why do you need this?" prompt
   - Tracks bypass frequency (shame mechanism)

## Anti-Patterns Avoided

✅ **Not demographic personas** - Defined by behavior (distraction patterns), not age/location
✅ **Not based on assumptions** - Synthesized from interviews and Reddit research
✅ **Scenarios are specific** - Actual moments with trigger/frustration, not abstract
✅ **Tensions identified** - Parent needs simple, Developer wants data (can't optimize for both)
✅ **Validation flagged** - Open questions about monetization and differentiation
