# Problem Framing: FocusFlow

## Problem Statement

Remote workers without dedicated office spaces lose 3+ hours daily to household distractions and context-switching, leading to missed deadlines, overtime work, and work-life boundary erosion.

## Target User

Remote knowledge workers (developers, designers, writers, analysts) who work from home full-time without a separate office space.

**Characteristics:**
- Work from bedroom, living room, or kitchen table
- Live alone or with roommates/family present during work hours
- Struggle with self-discipline around distractions
- Use Slack, email, and multiple browser tabs throughout the day
- Feel guilty about distracted time but can't seem to fix it

## Jobs to Be Done

- **Functional:** Complete deep work tasks without interruption from household or digital distractions
- **Emotional:** Feel productive and in control of my workday instead of scattered and reactive
- **Social:** Appear professional and reliable to colleagues despite working from a chaotic home environment

## Current Alternatives

- **Pomodoro timer apps** (Forest, Focus Booster) - Used occasionally, abandoned when they become annoying
- **Website blockers** (Freedom, Cold Turkey) - Set up once, disabled when "need" to access blocked site
- **Manual willpower** - Telling themselves "no distractions" - fails within an hour
- **Physical solutions** - Headphones, moving to coffee shop - not always available
- **Accountability partners** - Asking roommate to check on them - feels embarrassing

## Trigger Moments

- **Morning:** Starting work day, knowing distractions are lurking
- **Mid-morning:** After first distraction, feeling frustrated with self
- **After meetings:** Struggling to regain focus after context switch
- **Mid-afternoon slump:** Energy low, willpower depleted, most vulnerable to distractions
- **End of day:** Realizing worked 8+ hours but only got 4 hours of actual work done

## Key Assumptions

- Remote workers are willing to use blocking software during work hours
- The pain of lost productivity outweighs desire for unrestricted internet access
- Users will truthfully track their focus vs distracted time
- Desktop-only solution is acceptable (mobile not required for MVP)
- Users work primarily on computers (not paper-based work)
- Free tier is necessary to drive adoption (can monetize later)

## Success Criteria

- Users complete 4+ focused work sessions (90+ min total) per day
- Distraction time reduced by 40% within first week
- 60% of users return for second day of use
- Users self-report feeling more productive after 5 days of use
- Average daily active usage: 20+ days per month

## Open Questions

- How intrusive should blocking be? (Can they disable it mid-session?)
- What level of accountability is helpful vs annoying?
- Should we track specific websites or just "distracted time"?
- Do users want to see how they compare to others (social/leaderboard features)?
